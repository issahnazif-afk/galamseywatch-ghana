Rivers that once fed Ghana's towns are running brown, and floods keep catching communities unprepared. Two tools turn open satellite and weather data into plain-language alerts for every district.

GalamseyWatch

Spots new land disturbance and muddy rivers, then sends each signal to a person to check.

FloodWatch

Checks each district against its own limits for rain, rivers, dams and the sea.

Why it matters

About 60%

of Ghana's water bodies are polluted, CSIR-Water Research Institute scientists reported in 2026, with illegal mining a major contributor.

10,000+ NTU

turbidity recorded at intakes on the Pra and its tributaries, about five times what the plants are built to treat.

30,000+ people

displaced by the 2023 Akosombo and Kpong spillages in Volta, Eastern and Greater Accra, according to NADMO.

How each tool is designed to work

GalamseyWatch

  1. Satellites lookRadar every 6 days, through cloud. Optical images about every 5 days.
  2. AI flags changeCleared land, new pits and muddy water, compared with the past.
  3. People verifyAn analyst checks licences, reserves and imagery. Field teams confirm.
  4. Right people toldAgencies get detail. Communities get safe, general notices.

FloodWatch

  1. Forecasts arriveRiver flow, rainfall, soil wetness, waves and dam notices.
  2. Local limits appliedEach district has its own limits, tuned to past floods.
  3. Duty officer signs offThe level is explained by its cause and reviewed.
  4. Everyone reachedSMS, WhatsApp, voice, radio and app, through official channels.

What is real today

Real

  • Maps of all 260 districts and 16 regions
  • River systems, intakes and flood-prone places from public reports
  • The flood rule engine and the satellite screening script
  • Phone-friendly design that can be installed from the browser

Not real yet

  • Every score, alert, case and status on the maps
  • District limits, until calibrated with national agencies
  • The trained AI model, sign-in and message delivery

A satellite signal cannot show whether mining is legal. Official flood warnings come from NADMO, GMet and the Ghana Hydrological Authority. This prototype does not replace them.
